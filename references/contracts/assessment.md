# Assessment extension 3 for final schema 13

New guided reviews attach `assessment` version 3 to final 13. Readers preserve versions 1 and 2 and records without the extension; new guided writes require version 3. Reading a retained record checks its historical contract and selected-byte integrity rather than retroactively applying current write rules. Version 2 reads support the published interpretations from both earlier producers, including records without `history_incomplete`. Strict older readers need updating together with producers of a shared archive. No historical record is rewritten. For new follow-ups, verify the prior local archive before preparing the run under [re-review.md](../workflow/re-review.md).

- `scope` repeats `base`, `head` and `snapshot` and lists the inspected `delta`. Each delta entry has `detail`, observed `evidence_ids` from shared coverage and `sources` binding selected proof files for those observations. The presentation kind determines whether the verdict is a bounded follow-up. An ordinary review needs complete ordinary coverage to claim that scope.
- `previous` is null or `{review_id, source}` for the exact verified earlier result. Follow-ups require a previous result. Select an exact copy of its final JSON as evidence. The writer verifies the copy against the completed archive in the same logical scope. The retained result verifies the copy against its digest.
- `findings` covers each current finding and follow-up ID once. A row has `id`, `cause`, `relation`, `verification`, `resolution` and `reopening`. Use a stable cause key independent of path, line, title and mitigation. `relation` is `in_scope`, `affected_consumer` or `outside_scope`. Verification records `obligation`, reachable `trigger`, `consequence`, `consumer`, `question` and observed `evidence_ids`. Use null for unknown facts and for consumer and question when inapplicable. Outside the inspected delta a prior claim remains `not_reevaluated`.
- `resolution` is null for the normal resolution policy, one decision, or a nonempty array of distinct decisions. A version 3 decision is `{status, scope, authority, channel, source, conditions, authorization_id}`. Historical decisions omit `authorization_id`. Status is `accepted_debt` or `out_of_scope`, scope is `this_review` or `logical_scope`, and authority is `user` or `scope_owner`. Channel is `chat`, `comment`, `description` or `document`. Preserve each member's complete decision, source and conditions; do not merge sources or infer a new authority from a union. Check authority and compatibility before verification. A proposal without accredited authority is context. Accepted debt uses logical scope and keeps its conditions. An omission applies to the selected scope of the decision.
- `reopening` is null or `{reason, detail, condition, evidence_id, source}`. Reasons are `new_evidence`, `condition_changed` and `pending_gate`. Bind the source to an observed delta entry. Use bytes not already relied on for the prior assessment. Identify the exact prior condition when it changed. An accepted debt can reopen when a material change is demonstrated, including evidence of a new or changed gate.

A `source` is `{reference, sha256}`. Use an explicitly selected flat evidence file and the digest of its original bytes. A fragment may identify a decision in the file. Hashes bind the selected bytes. The reviewer establishes authority and material relevance. Keep raw source references out of the public comment.

In versions 2 and 3, every local `evidence/...` reference used by a delta observation has its exact descriptor in that delta's `sources`, including its fragment. A `still_valid` outcome requires a current confirmed finding. A `resolved` or `withdrawn` outcome requires at least one decisive current observation: sufficient static inspection or a completed check, excluding reused checks and fixture/environment failures. An inconclusive check alone cannot close a finding. Historical version 1 reads keep their original source and outcome rules; reading a legacy outcome does not establish that it meets the current evidence standard.

## Cumulative continuity

Version 3 retains `lineage` and `observed_hashes` from version 2:

- `lineage` reserves every published member with `{id, cause, survivor, title, last_status, resolution}`. `last_status` is the last material outcome: `confirmed`, `unresolved`, `rejected`, `resolved`, `withdrawn`, or explicit `unknown` when the selected legacy baseline cannot establish it. `not_reevaluated` or an omitted terminal row preserves that outcome. Omission never releases an ID, alias or cause for reassignment.
- Each member keeps its own complete resolution decision or decisions. Consolidation points members directly to a published survivor and preserves every applicable decision. A changed disposition or reactivation of a terminal member requires material reopening evidence for that member. Reordering identical decisions does not change the disposition.
- `observed_hashes` is exactly the union of the verified baseline's accumulated hashes and the sources used by the current assessment. Reformatting, changing a path, or skipping an intermediate reevaluation does not make previously used bytes new. The ledger binds provenance; it does not establish human authority or semantic materiality.
- The required version 3 boolean `history_incomplete` records missing historical evidence or authority history. Derive it conservatively when adopting an older baseline, including a version 1 observation without bound hashes or a chain without cumulative history, and preserve it in subsequent generations. Complete bindings for an immediate legacy delta do not establish complete ancestral history. Absence in an existing version 2 record is not a reason to reject its historical read or proof that its ancestry is complete. Incomplete history cannot prove `new_evidence`, even under another filename; a material `condition_changed` or `pending_gate` still requires decisive current evidence. Do not fabricate hashes or earlier authorizations to fill the gap.

The immediate verified version 3 baseline carries this bounded record, so follow-ups do not recursively load all old archives. Legacy baselines seed only the IDs and decisions actually available there. When adopting a version 1 result, a terminal outcome without decisive evidence seeds an unknown material outcome. Lost ancestors or unknown material outcomes are not reconstructed by inference; the current review must retain that limitation and cannot silently treat unknown as a confirmed active state. Keep applicable aliases direct when their survivor is in the current findings or tracking. A disclosed noncanonical historical ID may remain only in lineage when its exact identity and terminal status trace to the verified baseline; it does not need a redundant current tracking row.

When current evidence consolidates published findings into one cause, keep a published survivor and map every duplicate and inherited alias directly to it. Verify the survivor in the current delta. Its disposition must preserve the accepted decisions and conditions of every consolidated member; changing any applicable decision requires its material reopening evidence. An unchanged logical-scope decision keeps its original source without requiring initial recapture. A `this_review` exclusion expires across the entire group: moving it to another member does not renew it. Any new exclusion requires a current applicable authorization captured for this review.

## Authorization history

Version 3 requires `authorizations`, an exact cumulative ledger with entries `{id, member_id, review_id, scope, decision}`. IDs use `AU001`, `AU002`, and so on and do not derive from confidential content. `member_id` retains the original published member, `review_id` the authorizing review, and `scope` its pinned `{base, head, snapshot}`. `decision` retains the original complete decision without `authorization_id`. Each active resolution references its exact ledger decision and original member group.

Retain expired entries without rewriting or dropping them. A `this_review` authorization applies only in its original review. Its expiry does not expire an independent logical-scope debt authorized in the same note. New IDs, filenames, fragments, conditions, status or scope labels cannot renew an expired grant. A new authorization must have an applicable current authority, its current review and pinned scope, an initially captured source, and bytes not already observed in the baseline. A previously unseen digest alone does not establish authority. An inherited logical-scope authorization keeps its ID and source.

Import decisions demonstrated by a verified legacy baseline. A version 1 alias shares the displayed claim and disposition of its survivor and uses that survivor's original authorization ID. A chained version 2 baseline can include copies of the same disposition in several lineage rows. When its retained predecessor is available, select its exact final JSON as `previous.legacy_source`, a distinct flat evidence descriptor `{reference, sha256}` bound to the link in the version 2 result. This verifies one additional selected record from the baseline inventory. Distinct decisions and independently evidenced acts retain their conditions and authority, even when independently evidenced acts have identical text. When an identical alias disposition lacks independent provenance, carry its decision for the group and preserve `history_incomplete` to disclose the attribution limit.

Existing version 3 ledgers remain immutable. The retained reader also recognizes preliminary version 3 imports that used the earlier attribution rules. New writes apply the current import rules. For imported entries, use the review and scope of the first publication still supported by the selected bytes. Inactive ledger entries are verified against the hashed baseline JSON, while active decisions and genuinely new authorizations bind their selected source bytes.

## Confirming a code finding

In the assigned verification pass, cite the evidence for the obligation, the supported input or reachable caller that triggers it, and the concrete consequence. A requirement, published contract, applicable test, or traced caller can establish the obligation and trigger. A difference from the baseline shows what changed. Establish separately that the reviewed contract covers the scenario before confirming it, including for a nonblocking finding. Reject a candidate when its trigger is outside the supported scope. If a material in-scope question remains open, keep it unresolved and record the evidence gap. A decisive static trace is sufficient. Preserve accredited dispositions and their conditions when the confirmed behavior is accepted debt.
